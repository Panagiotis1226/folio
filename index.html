01 / about

Two jobs that turned out to be the same job

I’m a computer science graduate in Montréal who likes the unglamorous half of software: the part where it has to keep running.

At Polysleep I started on the customer-service side and moved into business systems and operations within months. Today I own the daily Shopify and CX reporting, run batch order fulfillment across Cin7 and ShipStation, and lead the team’s AI adoption — turning the repetitive parts of the job into automations someone else can maintain.

At home I run the infrastructure most people rent: a photo and file platform my family actually uses, load-balanced web services on Docker, game servers, and a Wazuh SIEM watching every endpoint on the network. When something breaks at 2 a.m., I’m the on-call.

Before software I led people — a soccer camp, a team roster, nightlife events — which is why I measure work by whether it got easier for whoever has to use it, not by tickets closed.

Daily sales, order, and customer-experience metrics pulled and shaped for the leadership stand-up, so the day starts from numbers instead of guesses.

Order-risk screening

A scan across every order in a date range that surfaces payment-risk patterns no single order page shows, ranked from high to cleared with a plain-language reason for each.

Shipment consolidation

Finds orders headed to the same recipient, combines them, and sets the packages per unit — a daily click-through chore reduced to one request.

Stock answers on demand

On-hand, open-sales, and backorder lookups straight from the data warehouse — one product or the whole catalogue — without anyone opening a dashboard.

customer voice ai skill · en / fr

Review replies that don’t sound canned

Drafts public replies in the brand’s voice and the reviewer’s language, with one hard rule: no two replies may reuse the same phrasing.

forecasting excel

A faster forecast workbook

Re-engineered a 60-plus-sheet, 300-plus-pivot planning workbook to recalculate faster — shared pivot caches, centralized links — verified value-for-value unchanged.

Every automation ships with an SOP, so it outlives the person who wrote it. Also in the toolbox: purchase-order PDFs parsed straight into spreadsheet rows, and the small recurring chores — folder scaffolding, hours logging — nobody should be doing by hand.
